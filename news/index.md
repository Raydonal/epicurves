# Changelog

## epicurves 0.0.0.9000

### Parametric bootstrap for the general engine

[`ga_bootstrap()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/ga_bootstrap.md)
simulates data sets from a
[`ga_fit()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/ga_fit.md)
result with
[`ga_simulate()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/ga_simulate.md),
refits them from the estimates and returns the replicates of any
statistic of the fit, with percentile and basic intervals
([`confint.ga_boot()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/confint.ga_boot.md)).
Missing values of the original data stay missing, and replicate seeds
are drawn from the current stream, so results do not depend on the
number of cores. To support fits with time-varying rates,
[`ga_simulate()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/ga_simulate.md)
gained a `varying` argument, and
[`ga_fit()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/ga_fit.md)
now returns the `optim` convergence code.

### Simpson quadrature of `Q_k` fixed at the start of each interval

[`sird_ga_transition()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/sird_ga_transition.md)
stored, as the first node of every interval after the first, the
resolvent of the previous interval instead of the identity: `deSolve`
records the pre-event value at an event time, so the row at `t_{k-1}`
held `Phi(t_{k-1}, t_{k-2})`. That node enters Simpson’s rule with
weight `h/3`, which made the error of
[`sird_ga_Qk()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/sird_ga_Qk.md)
decrease only in proportion to the step (2.7% with 10 substeps, 0.34%
with 80). With the node reset, the error is about 1e-5 against the joint
RK4 reference, limited by the `lsoda` tolerance. Only the prevalence
path
([`sird_ga_loglik()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/sird_ga_loglik.md),
[`sird_ga_fit()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/sird_ga_fit.md))
used this function; the incidence path and `ga_*()` never did, so no
result of the articles changes.

### Generic compartmental engine rewritten (`ga_model()` and friends)

[`ga_model()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/ga_model.md),
[`ga_event()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/ga_event.md),
[`ga_incidence()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/ga_incidence.md),
[`ga_prevalence()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/ga_incidence.md),
[`ga_loglik()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/ga_loglik.md),
[`ga_simulate()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/ga_simulate.md),
[`ga_fit()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/ga_fit.md)
and
[`ga_profile()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/ga_profile.md)
replace the earlier closure-based engine (`new_jump_model()`,
`general_ga_*()`, `seird_model()`, `sird_model()`), which is removed.
The new engine (`src/ga_generic.cpp`) handles any mass-action model with
all compartments explicit, integrates the mean, the resolvent and the
Lyapunov equation of the state-noise covariance jointly by RK4 (the old
engine obtained `Q_k` by Simpson quadrature on the stored ODE grid; the
relative errors of 0.3% to 3% that `check_Qk.R` first reported for it
came from the endpoint defect described above, not from the quadrature),
observes event incidence through cumulative event counters in an
augmented state, and allows piecewise-constant rate parameters. On SIRD
with daily cases and deaths it reproduces
[`sird_ga_loglik_inc()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/sird_ga_loglik_inc.md)
to within 2e-10 in the log-likelihood, for constant and time-varying
transmission (`test-ga-generic.R`).
[`ga_fit()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/ga_fit.md)
uses a Nelder-Mead search with relative tolerance 1e-8 followed by BFGS;
on a SEIRD example this reached the same maximum as two long Nelder-Mead
runs with about one fifth of the likelihood evaluations.

### Ill-conditioned innovation covariance no longer aborts incidence fits

The C++ Kalman log-likelihood (`src/sird_ga_fast.cpp`) called
`arma::inv_sympd()` on the innovation covariance after only a
determinant check. When that matrix passed the check but was
ill-conditioned, the call threw and aborted the surrounding
[`optim()`](https://rdrr.io/r/stats/optim.html), killing whole fits
(first seen in the reporting-probability profile of the time-varying
Paraíba fit at `p = 0.03`). The non-throwing overload is now used and
the evaluation returns the `-Inf` log-likelihood sentinel, so optimizers
penalize that point and continue. Fits that never hit the exception are
unchanged.

### Independent verification of Article 1’s inherited simulation tables

The Article 1 Scenario 1-5 SIRD-GA/SIR tables were transcribed from the
thesis rather than regenerated by this package. A reduced, disclosed
spot check (`J=10` replicates, not the manuscript’s `J=100`, owing to
real cost: ~70-130s per fit at `N=20,000` since Nelder-Mead needs the
full `maxit=1000`+ budget to converge reliably) was run for Scenario 1
at `N=20,000` using
[`sird_ga_fit()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/sird_ga_fit.md)
directly, with early-extinction trajectories discarded exactly as the
manuscript’s own stated protocol requires (an unfiltered first attempt
produced a spurious, wildly diverged fit, which traced to an extinct
simulated series, not a package bug - see below). Result: mean estimates
centred near the true parameters (lambda 0.637 vs true 0.6, gamma 0.294
vs 0.3, alpha 0.131 vs 0.1, p 0.545 vs 0.5, tau2 0.444 vs 0.4), with
MSEs roughly one order of magnitude above the manuscript’s J=100 table,
consistent with the much higher Monte Carlo noise expected at J=10 (3 of
10 replicates did not fully converge within the iteration cap and were
included as-is). This confirms the inherited table is in the right
neighbourhood and free of gross implementation bugs, without claiming a
full independent replication.

Initial implementation, built to validate and reproduce the results of
two companion manuscripts derived from the thesis.

### Findings made while validating against the thesis

- **Richards growth-curve sign correction.** The thesis’s Richards-curve
  equation (Chapter 3), transcribed literally, uses
  `exp(+beta*gamma*t)`; this drives the curve to 0 as `t -> Inf` instead
  of converging to the carrying capacity `K`, inconsistent with the
  standard Richards (1959) form and with the thesis’s own (correct)
  Gompertz/Logistic equations.
  [`richards_curve()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/richards_curve.md)
  implements the corrected form, `exp(-beta*gamma*t)`. This needs to be
  corrected in the Article 2 manuscript’s methods section.
- **Partition-point over-segmentation.** The flat “50% MSE reduction”
  split-confirmation rule described in the thesis can over-segment long,
  structurally stable phases, both in a re-simulation of the thesis’s
  own Scenario 1 and in the real Paraíba death series.
  [`fit_piecewise_ensemble()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/fit_piecewise_ensemble.md)
  exposes a `confirm_criterion = "aic"` option (a BIC-style,
  parameter-count-penalised alternative) that is markedly more
  conservative while still recovering the Scenario 1 change points to
  the same accuracy as the thesis’s own reported values. Recommended for
  Article 2 as a robustness improvement, and as a direct response to the
  2023 referees’ novelty concerns about the (non-piecewise) precursor
  paper.
- **Generalized compartmental engine + SEIRD-GA.** `new_jump_model()` /
  `general_ga_transition()` / `general_ga_loglik()` / `general_ga_fit()`
  generalize the hand-derived SIRD-GA machinery to arbitrary
  compartmental structure (numerical rather than analytic Jacobian).
  Cross-validated against the original SIRD-GA engine on identical data
  (log-likelihoods agree to within 0.03%). `seird_model()` demonstrates
  a four-compartment extension (adds an Exposed compartment); MLE on
  simulated data recovers R0 exactly (2.500 vs true 2.500) and
  lambda/gamma/alpha within 18%.
- **Permutation-test partition criterion, and its limits.**
  `fit_piecewise_ensemble(confirm_criterion = "permutation")` implements
  a formal, multiplicity-corrected hypothesis test
  ([`permutation_test_partition()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/permutation_test_partition.md)),
  following Matteson & James (2013)’s e.divisive logic but specialized
  to parametric growth curves. Found to be underpowered at a
  computationally feasible replicate count (B=29): p=0.23 for a genuine,
  obvious change point that both heuristic criteria detect easily.
  Documented as a genuine cost/power trade-off in Article 2, not hidden.
- **External nonparametric detectors over-segment this data class.**
  Benchmarked [`ecp::ks.cp3o`](https://rdrr.io/pkg/ecp/man/ks.cp3o.html)
  and [`ecp::e.divisive`](https://rdrr.io/pkg/ecp/man/e.divisive.html)
  against the piecewise ensemble: both are 2-4 orders of magnitude
  faster but substantially over-segment smoothly-varying growth curves
  (e.divisive: 8 spurious interior points on Scenario 1’s
  3-true-changepoint series), because they assume approximately
  piecewise-stationary data and growth phases are never actually
  stationary. A real, reportable finding, not a configuration error.
- **SIRD-GA real-data identifiability: two basins.** Fitting SIRD-GA to
  raw daily case counts is sensitive to day-of-week reporting artefacts;
  a 7-day centered rolling average
  ([`smooth_series()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/smooth_series.md))
  is recommended before estimation. On the smoothed Paraíba series, a
  5-start sweep
  ([`sird_ga_fit_multistart()`](https://raydonal.github.io/epicurves/reference/sird_ga_fit_multistart.md))
  reveals two distinct optima: a “fast-dynamics” basin (4/5 starts,
  loglik ~2947, R0~1.03, mean infectious period ~1.4 days,
  epidemiologically implausible) and a “slow-dynamics” basin (1/5
  starts, loglik ~2924, R0~1.03, mean infectious period ~4.5 days,
  matching the published estimates and known COVID-19 natural history).
  Within the fast basin, gamma and alpha trade off freely while
  gamma+alpha stays fixed, mirroring the thesis’s own appendix finding
  that gamma/alpha are estimated far less precisely than lambda or R0.
  Recommended for Article 1: report the multi-start summary and prefer
  the slow-dynamics basin on epidemiological grounds, rather than
  reporting an unconstrained MLE point estimate. See the SIRD-GA
  vignette for the full table and discussion.
- **Article 1’s Scenario 2/3 had silently substituted the thesis’s
  appendix design.** The thesis’s main-text Scenario 2/3 (Chapter 2)
  vary only the reporting rate `p in {0.7, 0.9}` at fixed
  `(lambda,gamma,alpha,tau)= (0.6,0.3,0.1,0.4)`, isolating
  under-reporting severity on its own. The manuscript draft’s “Scenario
  2/3” instead reused the thesis appendix’s distinct full-parameter
  regimes (different R0 values entirely), losing the thesis’s actual
  finding that reporting-rate severity, on its own, is not the dominant
  driver of estimation precision. Fixed: the appendix regimes are now
  correctly labelled Scenario 4/5, and the real p-sensitivity Scenario
  2/3 (SIR baseline and SIRD-GA) were inserted with exact numbers read
  from `capitulo2.tex`. A genuine table/figure-ordering bug (Scenario
  1’s table and confidence-band figure left physically stranded inside
  the “Scenarios 4-5” subsection) was caught and fixed in the same pass.
